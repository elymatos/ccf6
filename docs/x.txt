The workbench was rebuilt against the new artifact contract, and there's no typechecker installed for the runtime (no mypy/pyright/ruff), so I substituted import and compile checks alongside frequent pytest runs.

The baseline

Convergence carries position 0.64, shape 0.008. Form reaches 0.905 on shape with no learning — that's the local-feature shortcut §6.5 predicted, now measured. A later claim of relational learning has to beat 0.905, not zero.

Five findings from building it

The one I'd flag hardest: an unordered bag of pairwise offsets cannot separate T from ⊥, or ⊢ from ⊣. Object.relations() stores a multiset that's closed under reflection when the bar is symmetric, so those pairs store identically. The ordered walk separates all four. This sharpens ADR-0004 — part-relative structure buys translation invariance but is not sufficient to identify a figure — and it's now two premise tests.

Also: contrast can't segment figure from ground (the ranges overlap exactly at 0.5), so Ego visits a known Object's parts and segmentation is neither solved nor claimed; local pooling was wrong by default for colour and form; fan-in needed declaring per connection class; and a closure captured the contrast field before any figure was placed, silencing a whole Space in a way that looked like a wiring fault.

All five are in docs/journal/2026-09-08-architecture-foundation.md.

Two things you should decide

Artifacts are gitignored, so the workbench tests that need a current-contract run now skip rather than fail on a fresh clone. If you'd rather they always run, a committed fixture artifact is the alternative — I avoided it because a fixture lets the page and the contract drift apart silently.

The names are still provisional. Web, Schema and Index are throughout the runtime, the artifact contract and the workbench now, so settling them gets more expensive from here.

The code-review agent is still running; I'll report what it finds and fix anything real in a follow-up commit. Nothing is pushed yet.
