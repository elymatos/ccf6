### §7 Hub-and-Spoke Semantic Architecture
*Patterson · Lambon Ralph · Binder · Fernandino · 2007–2016*

A central question in semantic memory research is how the brain organises conceptual knowledge. The **hub-and-spoke model** (Patterson, Nestor & Rogers, 2007; Lambon Ralph et al., 2017) proposes a two-tier architecture. Modality-specific "spoke" regions (visual cortex for shape and color concepts, motor cortex for action concepts, auditory cortex for sound concepts) represent sensory and motor features of concepts. A transmodal "hub" region in the **anterior temporal lobe (ATL)** integrates these features across modalities to support unified conceptual representations that generalise across contexts.

Evidence comes primarily from **semantic dementia**: atrophy of the ATL produces a global collapse of conceptual knowledge across all modalities, precisely because the hub has been destroyed while the spokes remain partially functional. Patients lose the ability to understand what a robin is, despite being able to perceive robins visually.

Binder, Fernandino, and colleagues (Binder et al., 2016; Fernandino et al., 2016) proposed a complementary system: the **Default Mode Network (DMN)** — a distributed set of regions including the precuneus, angular gyrus, posterior cingulate, and dorsomedial prefrontal cortex — carries experiential abstraction. Where the ATL hub supports taxonomic category knowledge, the DMN hub supports event-level, emotional, and experiential abstraction. Binder's 65-dimensional experiential feature space organises around DMN regions.

In CCF, spoke-CMs have strong inbound connectivity from a single PERCEPTUAL or ACTION_MOTOR area. Hub-CMs have diverse inbound connectivity from three or more distinct systems, and their activation is stable across varied perceptual contexts. Two hub subtypes are distinguished: **taxonomic hubs** (ATL-analog) and **experiential hubs** (DMN-analog).
